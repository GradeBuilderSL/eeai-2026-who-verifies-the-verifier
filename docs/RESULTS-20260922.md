# Results — what we measured, 21–22 September 2026 (draft for the paper; English)

Companion to `REVIEW-SYNTHESIS-20260921.md` (six external reviews) and `smoke-tests-20260921/README.md` (every run
with its files). Everything below is reproducible from the committed scripts; raw H1/FR data live outside git in
`/data/PycharmProjects/project-memory-runs/`. Costs are in US cents/dollars of API use; nothing was run twice
unless stated. Claims are stated with the evidence they rest on and the limits that bound them.

## 1. Setting and the question

A multi-stage pipeline compresses a versioned source into an intermediate paraphrase; a downstream stage states a
claim; a verifier (an LLM) judges the claim against what it is shown. The submitted abstract asks when such
verification is evidence, states that agreement of models sharing a degraded intermediate is correlated rather
than independent, and promises to quantify what evidence anchoring recovers. We measured exactly that, on three
kinds of data: (i) H1's natural summarizer errors (AggreFact, 160 article families), (ii) two injected
restriction-drop seeds (20 self-authored, 23 model-authored, five corruption families), (iii) an external legal
benchmark with deterministic gold (fiscal-fr-bench, ICML 2026; 40 and 100 date-anchored questions over the
versioned French tax code).

## 2. Re-analysis of H1 (natural summarizer errors)

- H1's arms were *compressed intermediate* (≤5k chars) vs *full original* (≤10k), not "naive vs anchored"; labels
  are AggreFact's natural errors, not injected distortions. Paired transitions: 50 verdicts moved
  unsupported→supported, 11 the other way — a threshold shift toward accept, not a loss of discrimination
  (TPR .744→.863, FPR .250→.406, Youden J unchanged, LR− .342→.232).
- **Placebo-length control** (64 claims, mini): compressed text padded with unrelated prose to the original's length
  reproduces the original arm's false accepts (10 vs 11 of 32) without its correct-accept gain (24 vs 29 of 32).
  The false-accept rise is context-length yes-bias; the recall gain is the original's content. (n small; direction.)
- **Three judges on identical input** (mini / gpt-5.4 / deepseek, 64 claims): unanimous 73 %, correct when
  unanimous 93 % (per judge ~80 %); unanimous acceptance of a false claim 0/28 (compressed) and 2/32 (original).
  On natural summarizer errors, agreement is informative. The abstract's regime is *absent* here.
- Minimal-edit search (sentence deletion 0/20; qualifier-span deletion 1/20) finds no fragility on these claims:
  they are hallucinated details, not dropped restrictions.

## 3. Injected restriction drops (five families)

Original with one critical restriction span (+ decoy); corrupted intermediate = original minus the span; claims
date-free; the as-of date in the question. Judges: mini, gpt-5.4, deepseek-flash; one run each (drift ±1–2 cases
over three passes).

| | naive: accepts the false claim on the corrupted intermediate | anchored: rejects it on the original + as-of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| self-authored seed (20) | 12 / 8 / 9 | 19 / 19 / 20 |
| deepseek-chat-authored seed (23) | 14 / 7 / 9 | 18 / 23 / 22 |

Three judges unanimous and wrong on the corrupted intermediate: 5 of 9 unanimous (own seed), 7 of 10 (model seed);
on the original: 0 of 19 and 0 of 17. **The regime the abstract describes appears as soon as the intermediate drops
a restriction, and disappears when the judges see the versioned original.**

Family map (naive accepts, mini / gpt-5.4 / deepseek of 4, model-authored seed): cursor_ignored 4/3/4 ·
eras_merged 4/3/4 · interval_dropped 4/0/0 · cancelled_kept (rewrite operator) 7/5/4 of 7 · recorded_as_valid
(object-level claims, rewrite) 8/5/6 of 8. Two methodological rules learned: (a) for cancellations and
entry-into-force the corrupted intermediate must *assert* the stale state (deletion leaves a cue the judges use);
(b) a dropped restriction makes a claim false to a verifier only when the claim asserts a checkable wrong value
for the date — scope-only drops read as "broadened" (layered scope cases on real articles: 19/25 accepted even
against the original). Fragility distance d_flip is binary on single-restriction cases and gains variance on
layered ones (8 cases: 1 ×3, 2 ×4, censored 0).

## 4. External data with deterministic gold (fiscal-fr-bench, R3 "killer" questions)

Conditions per question — A: the model's ≤1500-char compression of the bounded version history; B: the version
in force today (static RAG); C: the version in force on the question's date; D: extract the asked figure per
version, dates from the version metadata, `resolve` picks the as-of value. Deterministic nugget scoring (strict).

| Answerer | A compressed | B current | C as-of | D extract-per-version + resolve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| gpt-5.4 (100 q) | 77/100 | 3/100 (benchmark 2.7 %) | 91/100 (benchmark frontier 99.1 %) | **40/40 · 99/100** |
| gpt-5.4-mini (100 q) | 52/100 | 3/100 | 90/100 | **39/40 · 99/100** |

Verifier = the same model judging its own answer against the evidence it came from — accepts WRONG answers:
A 11/23 (gpt-5.4), 36/48 (mini); B 6/97, 26/97; C 6/9, 10/10. Three independent judges (mini, gpt-5.4 re-judge, deepseek) over the 100 gpt-5.4 answers, v3 set (full texts):

| Condition | unanimous | unanimous accept of a WRONG answer | of a right one | accepts per judge (mini / gpt-5.4 / deepseek) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A compressed (96) | 65 | **9 of 23 (39 %)** | 53 of 77 | 85 / 69 / 81 |
| B current (100) | 44 | 2 of 97 (2 %) | 1 of 3 | 27 / 6 / 11 |
| C as-of (100) | 72 | 5 of 9 | 67 of 91 | 100 / 75 / 97 |

(v2 set, capped texts, for the record: 9/28, 2/97, 4/8.)

Readings. (1) The evidence condition, not the model, decides (3 → 90 of 100 for both models). (2) A verifier
sharing the evidence that produced an error does not see it; three judges certify a third of the wrong answers
on the compressed intermediate and almost none on the visibly stale version — **agreement is uninformative
exactly when the degradation is invisible in the evidence**. (3) Asking the model for one typed figure per
version and letting the data model own time is the most accurate condition measured (79/80 on the 40, 198/200 on the 100 — the one miss is shared by both models on art. 1605 bis, where a source version with an inverted validity range (start 2021-01-01, end 2020-12-31) exists; our rescoring rule drops the expiry of such versions, which let its older figure win over the correct version — a corpus quirk interacting with our fix, left as is rather than re-tuned), above being handed
the correct version (C): the deterministic layer is where reliability comes from.

## 4b. Who verifies the verifier — the deterministic layer does (`smoke-tests-20260921/fr_detector.py`, stored data only)

Comparing each answer's figure with the value the journal resolves for the date (condition D v4) as the verifier:

| Condition | wrong answers caught / total | right answers wrongly flagged | wrong answers the 3-judge panel unanimously passed | of which caught |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A compressed | 22 / 23 | 0 / 77 | 9 | **9** |
| B current | 96 / 97 | 0 / 3 | 2 | **2** |
| C as-of | 9 / 9 | 0 / 91 | 5 | **5** |

The single miss in A and B is the case where the resolved value itself was wrong (art. 1605 bis). Every wrong answer
that three LLM judges unanimously certified is caught by the resolved value, with no false alarms on right answers.

An evidence-only surface cue does **not** work: whether the compressed text still contains the applicable version's
start date does not separate wrong from right answers (wrong: 13 with the date present, 10 absent; unanimously-
certified wrong: 7 present, 2 absent). The compressed text keeps dates; the model misapplies them. So the signal
"when agreement is worthless" cannot be read off the evidence's surface — it needs the journal's resolved value,
which is exactly the deterministic layer the abstract points to.

## 4c. Predictive advice from recorded history (`smoke-tests-20260921/fr_predict.py`)

125 pairs of killer questions on the same article with anchors d1 < d2. From the vantage of d1 (the version history
built around d1), answer question 2 about d2: the model reads the full history; `resolve` on d2 runs over the
events extracted per version from that same history (D v4, stored). Scored with question 2's nuggets.

| | future version recorded in the d1 window (47 pairs) | future not recorded (78 pairs) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| model answers from the full history (gpt-5.4 / mini) | 38 / 39 correct | 5 / 6 "correct" by coincidence; 72–73 confidently wrong |
| `resolve` on d2 | **45 / 45** correct | 2 correct; **72 say "expired" (no basis — no answer)** |

Where the future is recorded, resolution beats the model by 6–7 pairs (7 pairs model-wrong/resolve-right, 0–1 the
reverse). Where it is not, the model still produces a figure almost every time (an invented future); the journal
declines. For advice this is the property that matters: every "will apply" either has a recorded event behind it
or is not said. (One run per model; the 78 unrecorded pairs also include cases where the later figure equals the
current one, which is why a few model guesses land.)

## 5. Small positive results on our own tool

- Event-level extraction → journal → `resolve` on 20 clean sources: 19/20 (mini), 18/20 (gpt-5.4), against 15–16/20
  when the model is asked for a status label. Status is computed, never extracted.
- The journal's as-of resolution with supersession chain is the mechanism behind condition C/D; the mechanism is
  prior art (Zep/Graphiti, Engram, TOKI, MemStrata, FiscalQA's versioned retrieval) — we claim the assembly and the
  measurements, not the mechanism.

## 6. What we do not claim

Not "runs in production" (integrated behind flags, not enabled). Not "more accurate" on H1 (a threshold shift).
Not novelty of bitemporal memory, as-of, supersession, provenance, fail-closed. Not our earlier cost numbers
(withdrawn). Not H3 (gold circular with the projection rule). Not a theorem. Not human-labelled gold yet (form
prepared, 30 cases). Not more than one run per condition unless stated. Not results on domains other than
English business/regulatory seeds and French tax law.

## 7. Harness errors we found in our own work (kept on record)

The 40k truncation that dropped the gold version from the history (3/40, 12/100); the 6k per-version cap that cut
the asked figure (7/40, 16/100); a header regex that failed on article numbers with spaces and on open-ended
versions; a projection clock that hid all events for as-of dates before 2000; old-franc thousands parsed as
decimals. Each was caught by a mechanical check ("is the asked figure in the input?", "do two models really give
identical results?") before any number reached a slide. The corrected runs are the ones reported.

## 8. Decision-ledger pilot (owner's new direction; synthetic corpus, 22.09)

See `decision-ledger-pilot-20260922/README.md`. Extraction of typed figures from messy owner notes 198/198 (linking must be
by metadata, not re-extraction: 116/198); population ledger predicts held-out outcomes 11/20 buckets (MAE 5.3) — equal to a
model given the same records, better than the model's opinion (8–10/20), no model call; resolved advice: 6 unsupported
figures vs 47 for free generation. Oracle ceiling 13/20 (MAE 2.25): outcomes are noisy and a hidden per-business trait is
unobserved — the individual-vs-population gap that the business's own history must close.
